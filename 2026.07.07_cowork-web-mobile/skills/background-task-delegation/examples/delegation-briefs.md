# Worked delegation briefs

Four briefs written in the shape of [../templates/delegation-brief.md](../templates/delegation-brief.md).
The first three are the usage examples named in the post; the fourth is the scheduled-task example.

Each one follows the same discipline: **point at the material, describe what done looks like, and put
the gate where something would otherwise leave the building.**

---

## 1. Quarterly spend reconciliation and variance memo

**The job.** Reconcile the quarter's spend and draft the variance memo.

**The material.** The finance exports for the quarter, the ledger, and last quarter's memo as a
format reference. Out of scope: anything outside the quarter's date range.

**What done looks like.**
- A reconciliation showing every line that does not tie, with the amount and the suspected cause.
- A variance memo drafted in the same structure as last quarter's, explaining the material variances.
- Complete when every untied line is either explained or explicitly listed as unresolved.

**Constraints I am sure about.** Same memo structure as last quarter. Material threshold as we
normally use it. Do not adjust any figures — flag them.

**Approval gates.**
- Ask me before: anything that touches the ledger; sending the memo anywhere.
- Do not ask me about: how to categorise a line that clearly matches an existing category.
- Leave drafted but unsent: the memo.

**Timing.** Now. I need to act on it before the close meeting.

## 2. Contracts folder to renewals tracker

**The job.** Turn the folder of contracts into a renewals tracker with the risks flagged.

**The material.** The contracts folder — every file in it, including the ones that are scans. Out of
scope: contracts that have already terminated.

**What done looks like.**
- A tracker with one row per active contract.
- Columns: counterparty, renewal date, notice period, owner, auto-renew yes/no, flagged risks.
- Risks flagged with the clause they come from, so I can check the flag against the source.
- Complete when every active contract in the folder has a row, and any contract that could not be
  read is listed separately rather than silently dropped.

**Constraints I am sure about.** Flag rather than interpret — I want to make the judgment calls on
what is actually risky. Do not contact any counterparty.

**Approval gates.**
- Ask me before: anything that leaves the tracker. Nothing goes to the counterparties or to legal.
- Do not ask me about: which column a piece of information belongs in.

**Timing.** Now; I need it before renewals planning next week.

## 3. Client deck from call transcripts and pipeline data

**The job.** Build tomorrow's client deck from the call transcripts and the pipeline data.

**The material.** The call transcripts for this account, the pipeline view, and our standard client
deck template. Out of scope: transcripts from other accounts.

**What done looks like.**
- A deck following the standard template, populated for this client.
- Every claim about the account traceable to a transcript or to the pipeline data.
- Complete when the deck runs end to end without placeholders.

**Constraints I am sure about.** Tomorrow's meeting is the deadline. Standard template. Do not invent
numbers — if the pipeline data does not support a slide, leave the slide's gap visible and tell me.

**Approval gates.**
- Ask me before: sharing the deck with anyone, including internally.
- Do not ask me about: slide ordering within the template's sections.
- Leave drafted but unsent: the deck stays a draft until I have reviewed it.

**Timing.** Now, finished by end of day. I will review tonight.

## 4. Monday client prep, scheduled for 6 am

This is the post's own scheduled-task example, and it is the cleanest illustration of where the gate
goes. Scheduled tasks run with no device online, so nothing of yours has to be awake at 6 am.

**The job.** Monday's client prep.

**The material.** The email threads with this client, the call transcripts, and recent news about
them.

**What done looks like.**
- A briefing doc covering the threads, the transcripts, and the recent news.
- A follow-up email **drafted but unsent**.
- Complete when both exist and I can review them over coffee.

**Approval gates.**
- Ask me before: sending the follow-up. That is the only thing that would leave the building.
- Do not ask me about: what to include in the briefing doc — that is the job.
- Leave drafted but unsent: the follow-up email.

**Timing.** Scheduled: Monday, 6 am. I need to act on it by the time the client call starts.

**Where I will be.** Not at a laptop when it runs. Questions should reach my phone; I will review on
whichever surface I am on when I sit down with coffee.

## The pattern underneath all four

1. **A specific pointer**, not a category. "The contracts folder", named unambiguously.
2. **A finished state**, not a procedure. Something you can look at and say yes or no to.
3. **One clear gate** at the point where the work would become visible to someone else — and nothing
   blocked before it, so the work does not stall waiting on a decision that was never yours to make.
4. **Timing anchored to when you need to act**, not to when you wrote the brief.
