# Pre-prompt brief

Fill this out **away from the computer** — dictated, typed into a notes app, or
recorded as a voice memo and pasted in later. The point is that when you sit down,
the model executes an already-decided vision instead of discovering it with you.

---

## What am I making?

<Artifact type: prototype / deck / landing page / one-pager / email / animation /
social visual / wireframe.>

## Who sees it, and what do they need to walk away with?

<Audience and their context level. A deck for people with no background needs
different structure than a prototype for two engineers.>

## Fidelity

- [ ] Wireframe — structure only, fastest, many ideas
- [ ] Styled — direction is chosen, now it should look like something
- [ ] Interactive — clicking, state, motion matter to the point being made

## What it should look like

Say this explicitly, or a default aesthetic gets chosen for you.

- Fonts: <named, or "brainstorm pairings with me">
- Colors: <named, or a moodboard to upload>
- Reference / moodboard images: <list what you will attach>
- Anything to avoid: <the generic look you keep getting>

## Real context to attach

- [ ] Brand files, logos, typography specs
- [ ] Existing screens or screenshots
- [ ] Repository connection, for components and existing screens
- [ ] External information the design depends on (web search, MCP connections)

## Structure I already have in my head

<Describe the layout. If you can't put it in words, draw it on paper and attach a
photo instead of writing this section.>

## How many options?

<Default: ten. Then remix the one or two that work.>

## What I will do by hand afterward

<Rearranging, deleting, editing text, resizing, recoloring — plan to do these with
direct editing rather than prompting.>
