# The ten-options remix loop

A worked pass through the practice described as: "Ask for ten options, then remix.
Most of them won't be good, and that's fine; one or two will."

## Round 1 — widen

Prompt for breadth, with the aesthetic direction already stated so the options vary
on structure rather than all drifting to the same default look.

```
Ten wireframe options for the onboarding flow's first screen.
Fonts: <display> / <text>. Palette: <hex list>.
Vary the structure, not the styling — different information hierarchies,
different amounts of content above the fold, different entry points.
Label them A through J and keep those labels stable.
```

Wireframes here, not styled comps: asking for wireframes is much faster and keeps
attention on high-level structure instead of visuals, which is what makes trying
many ideas quickly viable.

## Round 2 — judge

Look for the one or two that work. Most won't, and that is the expected yield, not
a failure of the prompt. Name *what* works in each, not just which letter:

- B: the single-question-per-screen pacing
- D: the persistent progress indicator down the left edge

## Round 3 — remix

State the hybrid directly, in the form the post gives:

```
I like option B and a little of option D. Give me five riffs that smoosh
those together.
```

Five, not ten — the field is already narrowed, and the job now is variation within
a direction rather than exploration across directions.

## Round 4 — raise fidelity, once

Only after a direction survives the remix round should styling effort go in. Apply
the design system, then look at it as a styled artifact.

## Round 5 — finish by hand

Rearranging, deleting, editing text, resizing, and recoloring go through direct
editing tools, not prompts: "Direct edits use no tokens, and small calls like sizing
and alignment are better eyeballed anyway."

## Working in parallel

You don't have to wait for a round to finish before starting the next thought.
Queue multiple messages at once, or keep talking while the previous turn is still
being worked on — the loop above is a logical order, not a set of hard barriers.

## When words run out

If you can see the hybrid in your head and can't describe it, draw it on paper and
upload a photo. If the problem is identifying *which* element you mean, click it
and speak into the comment box with device dictation enabled, instead of writing a
paragraph that identifies it.
