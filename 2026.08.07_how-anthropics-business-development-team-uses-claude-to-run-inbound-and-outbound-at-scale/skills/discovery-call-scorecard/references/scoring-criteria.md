# Setting the bar for pass/fail

The post specifies "an explicit pass or fail score on our criteria." This
reference is about the choices that sit behind that word *explicit* — the post
states the requirement, not how any particular team sets its bar, so treat what
follows as implementation guidance rather than reported practice.

## Why explicit matters

Commentary-only feedback lets every rep read their own call as fine. A verdict
does three things a paragraph of notes does not:

- it makes the standard the same for everyone,
- it makes disagreement about the standard visible and therefore fixable,
- it gives the "practice next" item a reason to be the one chosen.

## Choosing disqualifying criteria

Some criteria should fail a call on their own; most should not. A useful test:
would a call missing this be worth running again? If yes, it is disqualifying.

Keep the disqualifying set small. If four criteria can each fail a call
independently, almost every call fails and the verdict stops carrying
information.

## Handling partial credit

Transcripts are messy. A criterion is often *nearly* met. Two workable
conventions:

- **Met / not met, with a note.** Simplest, and it keeps the verdict crisp. The
  near-miss goes in the improvement list.
- **Met / partial / not met, where partial never passes a disqualifying
  criterion.** More informative, slightly harder to keep consistent.

Pick one and apply it across the team; mixed conventions make scores
incomparable.

## Keeping scores comparable over time

- Record the playbook version on every scorecard.
- When a criterion is reworded, expect the pass rate to move — that is the
  rewording, not the reps.
- Re-score a small sample of old calls after a playbook change if you need the
  trend line to mean anything.

## When the score is wrong

Record why, and feed it back into the skill — the same loop the team applies to
its other skills. Repeated mis-scoring on one criterion usually means that
criterion is not checkable from a transcript, and belongs in a human's review
instead.
