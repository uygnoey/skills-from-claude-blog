# Scaling timeline — Anthropic's test impact analysis service

Summarized from the post (author: Sachin Malhotra, Sep 14, 2026).

## Load drivers
- Engineers ship ~8x as much code per quarter as in 2021-2025; Claude authors ~80% of it and plays a
  large role in reviewing and approving PRs.
- Tests across the codebase grew ~10x with a nominal increase in engineers.
- CI jobs grew ~25x over six months (not every test runs on every PR).

## v0 design
- Deterministic listener (records results) + selector (chooses tests per PR from history).
- Single process: a running per-test history required a single writer, so it could not shard
  horizontally.

## Patches

| # | When | Patch | Lasted | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Oct (prior year), after two straight days of pages | Doubled the cores | ~70 days | Known to be fleeting; ownership of the service was murky. |
| 2 | Feb | Sharding: one writer per package, each package's state in its own shard + worker (code generated by Claude) | 29 days | The listener needed ordering per package, not globally. |
| 3 | Mar | Daily restarts after hitting memory limit by mid-afternoon most weekdays | < 1 day | Only four bugs found; swapping the memory allocator did nothing; profiling a loaded singleton felt too risky; restarts made the service drift further behind, and >1h lag lost job results. |

Between patches, a long-running internal Claude Tag session monitored the service, pinged at
>50,000 jobs of lag, and often argued for an overhaul.

## Redesign
- In-memory data store holds state; stateless listener workers append results to a journal.
- Small consumer rolls the journal into per-test history every few seconds; selector reads it.
- More expensive to run, easier to scale and profile.
- One engineer, three weeks (a year earlier: closer to a quarter).
- Claude did most of the tuning (journal size, worker count) largely autonomously.
- Result: hourly max of queued unprocessed job-result events went from growing week over week to flat.
