# Running hands-on AI workshops for small businesses

Lessons from Anthropic's six-week tour (with partner Tenex) of free half-day workshops in ten U.S. cities,
attended by more than 1,000 owners and operators.

## Who came
- Mostly companies with 5 to 50 employees, often in construction, manufacturing, logistics, trades, and
  professional services. Many owners are non-technical.

## What they wanted to do
- Run the business (about two-thirds of requests) and grow it (about a third). Reporting was the most common use case.
- Build their own tools for specific problems, e.g. bid prep from blueprints, part identification on the shop floor.

## Concerns to address up front
- **Accuracy:** coach owners to treat Claude like a new employee and check its work, for example by asking it
  to show its reasoning.
- **Data and governance:** in a survey of 503 decision-makers, data security was the most-cited barrier. Show
  human-in-the-loop review and sending, PII redaction, and a workflow registry; point to Anthropic's privacy and
  trust resources (Team and Enterprise plans don't train on data by default).

## Workshop design
1. **Partner locally.** Local partners such as chambers of commerce hosted the events.
2. **Assume chat-only experience.** Many attendees used Claude chat but had not tried Cowork, skills, or connectors.
3. **Center a guided build session.** It was the most valued part; agents feel intimidating until the first
   workflow runs. Nearly two-thirds asked for more hands-on help.
4. **Use the Claude for Small Business plugin** (built with QuickBooks, PayPal, HubSpot, Canva, DocuSign, and
   others) and the AI Fluency for Small Businesses course made with PayPal.
5. **Build peer learning in.** Attendees wanted a shared repository of peer use cases more than any feature;
   collect use cases from each session.
6. **Grow local trainers.** Some attendees went on to become consultants or trainers for other businesses.

- A second tour beginning in Boston on September 16.
