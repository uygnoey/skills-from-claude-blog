## What is this post?
Anthropic describes how it thinks about teaching people to use AI, on the occasion of launching Claude Academy — an education platform at academy.claude.com aimed at helping people learn to use AI safely and effectively. The post is less a product announcement than a statement of pedagogy: the stated belief is that AI instruction should increase agency and empower learners to expand their capabilities, and the curriculum design principles follow from that.

Five design principles are given — increase human agency, teach mindsets over specific behaviors, cover safe use holistically, make learning active, and use AI itself as a learning amplifier. The post also notes how Anthropic trains its own employees: a 4D AI Fluency Framework taught during onboarding, followed by continuing "ever-boarding" programs on capabilities, limitations, and human-agent collaboration.

## When is it useful?
- When you are writing internal AI training and keep defaulting to feature tours that go stale with the next model release.
- When adoption programs teach people to operate a tool but not to decide which work should be delegated in the first place.
- When "safe use" in your organization means prompt hygiene only, and ignores task selection, skill atrophy, and disclosure.
- When you need an onboarding-plus-continuing-education structure for AI rather than a one-time training session.
- When you want people to reach the point where they can use AI to learn anything else.

## Key points
- **Teach for agency, not for features.** Materials should be organized around real problems learners have, and should prompt them to reflect on which tasks stay human-owned and which get delegated.
- **Mindsets outlast instructions.** Because capabilities move quickly, durable framings are worth more than step-by-step feature guidance. Two given: "today's AI is the worst AI you'll ever use," and "verify in proportion to the stakes."
- **Safe use is broader than the chat window.** It includes choosing the right tasks, deliberately continuing to practice skills that matter to you so they do not atrophy, and stating explicitly how AI was used before sharing a document, analysis, or media with others.
- **Active learning is the delivery mechanism.** Courses carry practice exercises, reflection prompts, and room to experiment, so learners arrive at an approach that fits their own work rather than copying one.
- **Fluency compounds.** Once someone can work with AI well, that fluency becomes a general-purpose learning amplifier across any subject.
- **Internally, education is continuous.** New employees get the 4D AI Fluency Framework at onboarding; "ever-boarding" keeps going afterward on capabilities, limitations, and agent management. (The post links out to Claude Academy for the framework itself rather than defining the four D's inline.)
- **The platform itself.** Claude Academy offers recommended courses by interest, completion tracking and badges, and a Claude Academy Skill that recommends courses or learning paths based on how you work.
