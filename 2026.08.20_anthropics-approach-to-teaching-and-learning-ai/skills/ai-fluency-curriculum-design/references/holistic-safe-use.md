# Holistic safe use as curriculum

The source's third design principle says safe use reaches beyond the interaction
between a person and the model. Three areas are named: **task selection**,
**skill preservation**, and **disclosure**. Prompt-craft training normally skips
all three. This file turns each into teachable material.

---

## Task selection

The decision that precedes any prompt: is this a task that should be handed over
at all?

**What the learner should be able to do**
- Take a real week of their own work and sort it: delegate fully, delegate with
  review, keep.
- Say what makes an item belong in each column — not by rule, but by the
  consequences of getting it wrong and by who is accountable for the output.
- Recognize a task where the answer changes depending on stakes, and calibrate
  accordingly rather than applying one policy everywhere.

**Exercise shape**
Have learners bring five tasks from the past week. Sort. Then swap lists with
a partner and challenge two placements each. The disagreements are the lesson.

**Reflection prompt**
"Which item on your list did you place by habit rather than by judgment?"

---

## Skill preservation

Delegation has a cost that shows up later: capabilities you stop exercising get
weaker. The source frames this as mindfulness about continuing to practice
skills that matter to you, in order to prevent skill atrophy.

**What the learner should be able to do**
- Name skills that are load-bearing for their role or their own development.
- Deliberately keep some of that work manual, and be able to say why the cost of
  slower output is worth it.
- Distinguish a skill worth preserving from a task that is merely familiar.

**Exercise shape**
From the "delegate fully" column above, ask the learner to move exactly one item
back to "keep" purely on skill-preservation grounds, and to write the
justification in one sentence.

**Reflection prompt**
"If you delegated this for a year, what would you no longer be able to do
yourself — and would that matter?"

---

## Disclosure

The source is specific: state explicitly how AI was used in producing a
document, analysis, or piece of media *before* you share it with others.

**What the learner should be able to do**
- Write a one- or two-line statement of how AI was involved in a specific
  artifact — what it drafted, what it checked, what the person verified.
- Judge who needs to know: reviewers, downstream users, external stakeholders.
- Attach the statement as part of producing the work, not as an afterthought
  when asked.

**Exercise shape**
Take an output the learner produced with AI assistance this week and draft the
disclosure they would have attached. Compare against what was actually sent.

**Reflection prompt**
"Would the person who received this have wanted to know? What would they have
done differently?"

---

## Sequencing

These three are cumulative and belong before, not after, interaction technique:

1. **Task selection** — decide whether to delegate.
2. *(interaction technique lives here)*
3. **Skill preservation** — decide what you keep doing yourself anyway.
4. **Disclosure** — say what happened, before it goes out.

Teaching prompting first implies the delegation decision has already been made.
