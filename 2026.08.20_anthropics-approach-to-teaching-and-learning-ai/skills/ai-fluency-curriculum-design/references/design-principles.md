# The five design principles

The design principles Anthropic states for its AI education materials, each
expanded into what it rules in and what it rules out when you are actually
drafting a curriculum.

---

## 1. Increase human agency

**Stated as:** materials focus on solving real-world problems rather than
teaching features; learners are encouraged to reflect on which tasks should
remain human-owned versus delegated to AI.

**Rules in**
- Units organized around a task the learner already owns.
- An explicit delegation decision that the learner makes and can defend.
- Success measured as new capability, not coverage.

**Rules out**
- Modules titled after product surfaces.
- Implicit "delegate everything you can" framing.
- Walkthroughs where the learner never makes a judgment call.

---

## 2. Mindsets over specific behaviors

**Stated as:** given rapid AI advancement, emphasize durable mindsets rather
than feature-specific instructions.

**Named mindsets in the source**
- "Today's AI is the worst AI you'll ever use."
- "Verify in proportion to the stakes."

**Rules in**
- Framings that stay correct across model releases.
- Teaching the learner to re-test their own assumptions on a cadence.
- Calibration as a skill: how much checking is warranted, given what is at risk.

**Rules out**
- Numbered click-paths as the substance of a lesson.
- Absolute verification rules ("always check every output") that learners
  quietly abandon because they are unworkable.
- Presenting a current limitation as a permanent property of AI.

---

## 3. Holistic safe use

**Stated as:** beyond user-AI interactions, the curriculum addresses task
selection, skill preservation, and ethical disclosure of AI usage to
stakeholders.

**Rules in**
- Choosing what is appropriate to hand over in the first place.
- Deliberately continuing to practice skills that matter to the learner.
- Saying explicitly how AI was used before sharing an output.

**Rules out**
- "Safe use" as a synonym for prompt hygiene or data handling alone.
- Treating disclosure as a legal checkbox rather than a habit taught in the
  same breath as producing the work.

Expanded: [holistic-safe-use.md](./holistic-safe-use.md).

---

## 4. Active learning

**Stated as:** courses include practice exercises, reflection prompts, and
experimentation, to help learners discover personalized approaches.

**Rules in**
- Practice on the learner's own material.
- Reflection questions that require a decision, not a recall.
- Deliberate space to try something the instructor did not prescribe.

**Rules out**
- Read-only modules.
- A single "correct" workflow presented as the answer.
- Exercises with sample data the learner has no stake in.

---

## 5. AI as a learning amplifier

**Stated as:** once users develop AI fluency, they can use Claude as a learning
partner across any subject domain.

**Rules in**
- Treating fluency as a means, and naming the further learning it unlocks.
- A closing unit that points outward: use this to learn the next thing.

**Rules out**
- Ending the curriculum at tool proficiency.
- Framing the product as the terminal subject of study.

---

## Program shape

The principles above describe content. The source also describes structure:
a fluency framework taught during **onboarding** (internally, the 4D AI Fluency
Framework), followed by **"ever-boarding"** — continuing programs on AI
capabilities, limitations, and best practices for human-agent collaboration.

The source links out to Claude Academy for the 4D framework itself rather than
defining the four D's inline; consult the original for the definition.
