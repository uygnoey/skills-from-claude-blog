# What certification means for customers

The Claude Certification Program is designed to give enterprises confidence in the partner they are
selecting. Three properties carry that confidence.

## 1. Validated capability, not course attendance

- Every exam is **proctored** and **identity-verified**.
- Each exam was designed by subject matter experts, **including Anthropic's own Applied AI team**,
  to reflect real work that's done in the field.
- A certification establishes that this individual has **demonstrated the skills their role
  demands**.

## 2. Credentials matched to role-specific skills

Each credential maps to one of the four roles that put Claude into production:

| Credential | Role it maps to |
|---|---|
| Claude Certified Associate: Foundations | Practical, everyday use of Claude on Claude-related projects — consultants, project leads, business and technical roles |
| Claude Certified Developer: Foundations | Engineers building applications with Claude: the Claude API, tool use, agent development |
| Claude Certified Architect: Foundations | Solution architects who design and build agent systems with Claude |
| Claude Certified Architect: Professional | Integration architecture, governance, and evaluation at large-enterprise scale |

The stated design intent: **the buyer knows what a certified person was tested on.**

## 3. Network tier as a firm-level signal

The Claude Partner Network describes a firm's demonstrated capacity. Tier standing **combines
certified practitioners with deployed customers and public customer references** — three different
things, not one.

## Badges

- Passing an exam produces a **digital badge through Credly by Pearson**.
- Completed certifications are recognized with **partner badges**, which show demonstrated expertise
  in a specific product area.

Because certification is per-individual and identity-verified, asking for the badges of the named
people who will work on your systems is a stronger check than a firm-wide total.

## Why the rigor matters

Certified practitioner counts determine a firm's standing in the Claude Partner Network and are
relevant when companies vet who will work on their systems. A credential can only support those
decisions if the assessment is rigorous and validates a person's applied skills.
